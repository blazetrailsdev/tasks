## Context

`tasks/src/ranking.ts` (431 lines) is the ready queue: `claimable()`,
`nextBundle()`, `bestBundle()`, `comparePriority()`, `listFiltered()`,
`storiesTouching()`. It is pure functions over an in-memory `Index` because it
was ported verbatim from the pre-SQLite CLI — the file says so, and says the
shape is what made the equivalence gate hold by construction.

In trailmap these become scopes and class methods on the models, which is
where Rails puts them and where the associations already are.

The file documents the naming collision to resolve carefully: the ready queue
is called `claimable()` rather than `ready()` because `Story.ready()` is
generated by the `enum` and means only `status = 'ready'` — a much weaker
condition than "ready, under an active RFC, with every dep resolved".
Conflating them "would hand agents unclaimable stories", so keep the two names
distinct on the model too.

Both gates are load-bearing and must survive the move (`ranking.ts:141,150`):
a story is claimable only if its own status is `ready` **and** its RFC is
`active`.

## Acceptance criteria

- `Story.claimable()` is a scope/class method returning the same set, in the
  same order, as `ranking.ts`'s `claimable()`.
- Bundle packing (`nextBundle`, `bestBundle`, the knapsack) lives on the model
  layer with the same budget semantics.
- `Story.ready()` (the enum-generated status scope) and `Story.claimable()`
  remain distinct, and a test asserts they differ for a `ready` story under a
  non-active RFC.
- Priority ordering keeps its documented contract: lower N first, unprioritised
  last, ties stable in index order.
